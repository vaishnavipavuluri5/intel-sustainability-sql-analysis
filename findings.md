# Findings and Recommendations

## Part 1: Organizing and Understanding the Data

I notice more newer devices being repurposed than older ones. This might indicate that older devices are more difficult to repurpose as they require more maintenance and cleaning and they take more time and effort to be
made adequate for reusage.

## Part 2: Key Insights

Intel’s repurposing program saved around 25.74 kWh of energy per device per year, which totals to around 15.49 million kWh of estimated annual energy savings across the 601,740 devices. This is similar to the
annual electricity consumption of around 1,475 average households in the U.S. The program saved around 6,768.42 metric tons of CO2 emissions, which is similar to the annual emissions of around 1,471 gasoline-powered vehicles. I personally found the comparison to car emissions particularly impactful because it illustrates how repurposing
existing devices can contribute to significant reductions in emissions. These savings in energy and reduction in emissions give light to the environmental benefits of extending the lifespans of existing devices, which in turn can reduce the need for more manufacturing and can minimize electronic waste.

## Part 3: Identifying Trends and Maximizing Sustainability

Laptops contribute the most to energy savings and CO2 reduction. Repurposed laptops save approximately 25.80 kWh of energy and 11.28 kg of CO2 per year, compared to 25.62 kWh and 11.19 kg for desktops. Though the average savings per device are similar, Intel repurposed a significantly greater number oflaptops than desktops.
Since they repurposed 408,064 laptops and only 193,676 desktops, the number of devices repurposed likely plays a crucial role in contributing to energy savings and CO2 reduction.

I notice that as device age increases,the number of devices repurposed decreases, while the average annual energy savings per device increases. Perhaps repurposing older devices provides greater energy savings per device, though newer devices make up a larger portion ofIntel’s repurposing program.

Carbon intensity impacts the total CO2 savings from repurposed devices because reducing electricity consumption in regions that rely more on fossil fuels can prevent more emissions than in regions that use cleaner energy sources. Though all three regions have similar average energy savings, Asia has the highest average CO2 savings per device of 15.47 kg annually, compared to North America’s 10.28 kg and Europe’s 6.44 kg. North America has the highest total CO2 savings because it repurposes the greatest number of devices.

## Part 4: Data-Driven Recommendations

### Key Patterns

1. Older devices provide the highest average energy savings
(48.02 kWh) and CO2 reductions (21.01 kg) per device annually.
2. Newer devices are repurposed most frequently (317,191
devices), despite having the lowest average annual energy
savings (19.07 kWh per device).
3. Mid-age devices contribute significantly to overall energy
savings, with 264,310 devices repurposed and an average of
32.04 kWh saved per device annually.
4. Older devices are underrepresented, with only 20,239
repurposed devices, despite their greater environmental
benefits from repurposing.

### Recommendation for Intel

I recommend that Intel prioritizes repurposing more mid-age and older
devices that have sufficient remaining lifespans to be functional.
Through the analysis, it was found that older devices save an average of
48.02 kWh of energy and 21.01 kg of CO2 annually per device,
compared to only 19.07 kWh and 8.33 kg for newer devices. However,
older devices are a smaller portion of Intel’s repurposing program thus
far, and there is an opportunity to increase environmental benefits by
putting forth more effort in this device category.
I recommend that Intel should also continue repurposing mid-age
devices, as they offer relatively high energy savings of 32.04 kWh
annually per device, and they are available in larger quantities than older
devices. Intel could maximize energy savings and CO2 reductions by
making efficient use of mid-age and older devices.
